1. WHAT SERVD IS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

An all-in-one restaurant and food-business operating system built for the
Philippines. One account covers the whole business: QR menus at the table, a
cashier POS, a kitchen display, an online ordering website, delivery,
payments, inventory, accounting, HR and marketing.

  Who it's for     Restaurants, cafés, carinderias, food parks, milk tea
                   shops, bakeries, catering, cloud kitchens, and retail
                   sellers of packaged goods
  Where it runs    Any web browser — Android tablets, iPads, phones, laptops,
                   desktops. Nothing to install
  Money            Philippine Peso, stored in centavos so nothing rounds twice
  Time             Every "today", day boundary and printed time is Manila
                   wall-clock, wherever the server is
  Languages        English interface, per-item menu translations for diners
  Data isolation   Enforced at the database level (row-level security), not
                   just in the application

The business model, in one line

There is no monthly subscription. ₱499 activates a restaurant's online
ordering; every other paid feature is bought once and owned for good. The only
recurring charge in the entire product is the content scheduler at ₱499/month.
Servd takes no commission on sales.

4. HOW A DAY ACTUALLY RUNS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Order types — five names, used identically on every screen

🍽️ Dine-in (at a table) · 🥡 Takeout (ordered at the counter, waits for it) ·
🛍️ Pickup (ordered ahead, comes to collect) · 🛵 Delivery · 🏍️ Third-party
(GrabFood, Foodpanda)

Takeout and pickup are deliberately separate — the kitchen assembles them
differently.

Lifecycle

pending → new → preparing → done → closed, plus cancelled.

An order leaves the boards only when it is both paid and cooked. A takeout
order paid up front stays on the kitchen display until the food is made.

Four ways an order arrives

  1. QR dine-in — the diner scans the table QR, browses on their phone, orders
     with no app and no account. Live tracking, call waiter, request bill,
     promo codes, tips, feedback afterwards.
  2. Online ordering website — pickup and delivery, at a Servd URL or the
     restaurant's own domain. Map location picker, scheduled orders, payment
     proof upload, cart recovery, live tracking. Online orders can accept
     themselves after a wait the shop sets (10 seconds by default, off unless
     switched on), so a customer isn't left waiting for a confirmation nobody
     at the counter has time to tap. Dine-in QR orders and advance orders are
     never auto-accepted.
  3. Counter / POS — the cashier punches it with the full menu, modifiers,
     variants, discounts and any order type.
  4. Third-party — recorded so Grab and Foodpanda sales sit in the same
     reports as everything else.

Roles

  Role            Opens
  admin (owner)   Everything
  manager         HR and scheduling
  cashier         Cashier POS, shift summary, closed orders
  kitchen         Kitchen display only
  merchant        The "Incoming Orders" screen only

Plus a separate employee portal where staff see their own payslips, schedule,
attendance and leave.

5. THE CASHIER POS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The main service screen. Updates live — new orders appear without refreshing.

Orders — tables and open orders on one board · accept/decline incoming QR
orders · create an order at the counter · add items to an open order · edit
with manager approval · void an order or a single line with a required reason
and a void PIN · void an already-closed order (reverses the money, stays in
the audit log) · reopen a closed order · mark served · closed & voided list
for the shift

Mark a dish sold out from the till. A 🚫 Sold out button turns the item grid
into the switch: tap a dish to take it off the menu, tap a sold-out one to put
it back. It comes off the website and the QR menu immediately. Built because
lechon runs out at seven on a Saturday and the owner is not in the shop. If
the dish ran out by count rather than by hand, the till says so plainly
instead of letting the button look broken.

Payments — Cash · Card terminal · GCash · Maya · Bank transfer · cash tendered
→ change due with suggested notes · split payments · split bills · tips ·
Senior Citizen and PWD discounts · gift cards and store credit · loyalty
redemption · the customer's payment screenshot viewable from the order card

Cash handling — cash-out with a note · open drawer ("no sale") · shift notes

Printing — bill · receipt · kitchen ticket · end-of-shift summary

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. PAYMENTS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

At the counter: Cash, Card terminal, GCash, Maya, Bank transfer.

Online: the customer scans the restaurant's own GCash, Maya or bank (InstaPay
/ QRPH) QR, sends the money, and attaches the reference and a screenshot.
Staff confirm it against the account before settling — a screenshot alone
never closes a bill. The money lands directly in the restaurant's account.
Servd never holds it and adds nothing on top.

The card gateway was retired. Connected PayMongo/Xendit accounts redirected
the diner off-site to pay by card. Almost nobody used it and the extra hop
confused customers who already know how to scan a QR. Restaurants that bought
it keep it; it is never sold again.

Third-party orders show one button — "Picked up — settled by <platform>" —
instead of Cash/GCash/Card. It gets its own line in the shift breakdown,
Z-report and accounting; the drawer never opens; discounts, points and gift
cards are hidden (the app charged the customer and ran its own promo).
Recorded at full ticket value, not net of commission — commission is a cost
against the remittance, not a discount the restaurant gave.

Pay before the food is made. For counters where the customer orders, pays,
then sits down. The till leads with Take payment and nothing reaches the
kitchen until the money is in. It only decides which button is the big one —
the cashier can always send unpaid, or take payment on one that wasn't going
to be.

Card surcharge. An optional fee (e.g. 3.5%) added on top for counter card
payments, worked out on the server from the saved rate, shown before the
customer taps, printed as its own line with the rate on it. On a split bill it
applies only to the card portion. No loyalty points on the fee.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. THE KITCHEN DISPLAY
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Live tickets, colour-coded by how long they have waited — green fresh, amber
dragging, red genuinely late. Realtime updates with a polling fallback, so it
keeps working on flaky Wi-Fi.

  - Audible new-order chime, mutable, with a visible prompt when the browser
    is blocking sound (browsers need one tap before they will play audio)
  - Tap individual lines to tick them off as they are plated — struck through
    on every tablet, so the pass sees what is still to come on a five-item
    ticket
  - History of today's finished tickets, with the ability to bring one back
  - Order type, customer name, item notes and modifiers on every card
  - Optional delivery address on the ticket, for kitchens that batch by zone
  - Optional kitchen ticket printing instead of a screen
  - Offline mode (unlock) — keeps accepting taps with no connection and syncs
    when it returns

Extra rounds. A table that has been served and orders more used to be
invisible here: the ticket was finished, so the extras landed on the bill and
nowhere else, and somebody had to tell the kitchen to go and reopen it. Now
the ticket comes back on its own, the first round already ticked off so what
shows unticked is the extra, with an "Extra order" banner and the chime
ringing as it would for any new ticket.

8. SHIFTS AND END-OF-SHIFT REPORTING
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A shift is one cashier's turn at the till. It opens by itself the first time
they settle an order — nobody presses "start".

Per-cashier, not per-day. Each summary counts only that cashier's takings.

Shifts survive midnight — bounded by how long they have run (up to 16 hours),
not the calendar date, so a 6 PM–2 AM shift stays intact. One nobody signs out
of closes automatically and is flagged "not signed out".

The Z-report shows the cashier and start time, orders paid, sales by payment
method, discounts, expenses, cash collected, cash taken out, expected in
drawer, net, and the day's counter trade underneath for context — clearly
marked as not part of this cashier's drawer. It prints as a real ESC/POS
document, then End shift & sign out closes the shift and logs them out.

Shift history keeps every transaction, totals by method and by order type, and
the drawer figures, for 48 hours. Only the shift log is cleared; orders,
payments and accounting records are never touched.

Online payments count in shift totals but are excluded from the drawer figure
— nobody put that money in a till.

9. MENU MANAGEMENT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - Categories and items with photos, descriptions, prices, and videos
    (uploaded or a YouTube/Vimeo link)
  - Modifier groups shared across items, with required/optional and min/max
    rules — and one order set once, so every dish asks Size → Flavour → Add-
    ons in the same sequence instead of whatever order the database returned
  - Individual modifier options can be marked out ("86 the add-on")
  - Variants / sizes with their own prices and per-size stock
  - Dietary tags, daily servings limits, food cost per item (feeding COGS and
    profit)
  - Counter-only items — punchable at the till, never shown online or on the
    QR menu, and refused if a web order names one. For takeaway boxes charged
    by size, an add-on somebody wants three of, staff meals, and dishes that
    don't survive a delivery ride. A category whose items are all counter-only
    disappears from the storefront entirely.
  - Sort order, per-item translations, browser-side photo compression
  - AI menu import — photograph a printed menu and correct the draft
    (currently switched off; it burns API credits per import)

10. PRINTING AND HARDWARE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Four transports, set per restaurant:

  Method          How it works
  Network / USB   ESC/POS printer at a fixed station via a small local print-
                  bridge agent. Most reliable
  Cloud           The printer polls Servd for jobs (Star CloudPRNT, Epson
                  Server Direct Print). Works from any device including iPad
  Bluetooth       Web Bluetooth to a BLE ESC/POS printer. Chromium on
                  desktop/Android only — not Safari or iOS, and not inside the
                  Facebook, Messenger, Instagram or TikTok in-app browsers
  OS dialog       The HTML ticket through the system print dialog / AirPrint.
                  Device-agnostic fallback

A second kitchen printer can run on a different transport from the till —
including its own Bluetooth pairing — so the pass gets its ticket without a
print dialog opening at the counter.

Documents: bill, paid receipt, kitchen ticket, end-of-shift summary. 32-column
thermal, with name, address, phone, website, custom footer, optional VAT
breakdown, and a QR to order online. All times are Manila.

Optional receipt lines — who the order is for (name, number, and the delivery
address a rider actually reads) · cash received and change on cash sales · VAT
breakdown, off for non-VAT sellers.

Cash drawer plugs into the receipt printer and opens on a pulse: never / on
cash payments (default) / on every payment. Not available through the OS print
dialog, which cannot send control codes.

11. INVENTORY
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Two ways of keeping stock, usable together. Per product for shops selling
finished goods — one unit off the shelf per unit sold. Per ingredient for
kitchens — recipes define consumption, deducted when the order is fulfilled.

Both share: stock movements (sale, waste, adjustment, count, received, usage)
· weighted-average cost · suppliers · purchase orders with receiving that re-
averages cost · auto-reorder suggestions from 30-day velocity with one-tap
draft POs · low-stock flags and an optional SMS alert · auto out-of-stock ·
stock counts, waste write-offs, restocking · COGS reporting.

Overselling protection: sellable stock is what's on hand minus what's already
promised to orders taken but not yet fulfilled, so the last unit can't be sold
twice.

12. ACCOUNTING AND ANALYTICS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Accounting — gross sales / sales collected, order count, discounts, expenses,
net profit · sales by payment method · VAT (12%) with Senior/PWD exemption ·
Profit & Loss with COGS from inventory · daily sales (Z-report) · every ticket
in the period with a total that reconciles exactly to the headline figure ·
voided tickets listed separately · expense recording. Periods: today, this
month, last month.

Every revenue figure in the app means the same thing: money actually
collected, timestamped when the payment happened, bucketed in Manila time. The
dashboard, accounting and the Z-report use one definition.

Dashboard — pending acceptance, revenue today, orders today, open orders,
average rating, recent orders, low-stock warnings, and a "Today at a glance"
panel (best seller, busiest hour, low stock, 7-day rating).

Analytics — revenue, order count, average order value, revenue by day, best
and worst sellers, payment mix, peak hours, rating trends.

17. HOW IT'S BUILT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - Next.js 15 (App Router) · TypeScript · Prisma on PostgreSQL (Supabase) ·
    Tailwind · Vitest (710+ tests)
  - Tenant isolation at the database level. A tenant-scoped client for
    restaurant data; a service-role client only where identity itself is being
    resolved or a session-less webhook is running
  - Manila time goes through one module, so a server in UTC never prints the
    wrong hour on a receipt
  - Pure logic is separated from I/O — rules live in tested pure functions,
    the server layer does the reading and writing
  - Migration-lag safety is the house rule. New columns ship as hand-run SQL
    and are read in their own best-effort query, never folded into a shared
    one. The reason is a real incident: one new field added to a shared read
    made the whole query fail on a database that lacked the column, the catch
    returned defaults, and a settings page then saved those defaults over live
    payment and delivery configuration. A failed read must never become a
    successful overwrite

Two ways in:

19. KNOWN GAPS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Stated plainly, because knowing what is not there is part of knowing the
product.

  - Lalamove has no working adapter. The delivery layer is provider-agnostic
    and ServdGo has a real one, but a Lalamove API key will not book a rider —
    the generic REST template's auth, endpoints, field mapping and webhook
    signature are all placeholders. Lalamove works today as a deep link: staff
    tap through to Lalamove's own app with the details pre-filled, and status
    stays manual.
  - A printer-only kitchen reprints the whole ticket when extras are added,
    not just the new items. Fine on a display; a double-cook risk on paper.
  - Partner demo storefronts carry no Servd branding, since the demo pages
    don't pass the branding flag.
  - AI menu import is switched off and SMS runs on metered credits — neither
    is a one-time unlock, and both cost real money per use.

20. THE QUESTIONS THAT GET ASKED MOST
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Do customers need an app? No. Scan a QR or open the website in any browser.

Do I need special hardware? No. A thermal printer and cash drawer are optional
but supported.

Do I pay monthly? No — ₱499 activates your online ordering and it's yours for
good. Walang monthly bayad. Only the content scheduler is monthly, and only if
you want it.

Do you take a percentage of my sales? No. Customers scan your own GCash, Maya
or bank QR, so the money goes straight to you.

If I stop paying, do I lose what I bought? There is nothing to stop paying.
One-time unlocks are owned, not rented.

Can two cashiers work at once? Yes. Each has their own login and shift, so
takings are counted separately. Any cashier can still settle any order —
shifts decide whose money it is, never who may serve a table.

Does it work if the internet drops? The kitchen and cashier keep working
offline once that unlock is bought, and sync when the connection returns.

Why doesn't the shift summary show the whole day? Because it is that cashier's
drawer, not the restaurant's day. The day's total is underneath for reference,
marked as not part of the drawer.

Why is my dashboard revenue different from what I wrote down? Every figure in
Servd means money actually collected. A hand-written tally usually also
includes voided tickets and unsettled orders. Accounting lists every ticket in
the period, with voided ones separately, so the difference can be found line
by line.
